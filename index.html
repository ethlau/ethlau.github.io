Erwin T. Lau

Researcher, Nara Women's University, Japan

Nara Women's University

Nara 630-8506, Japan

I am a computational and theoretical astrophysicist. I study galaxy clusters and groups, the most massive objects in the Universe, and the hot gas in and around them.

My current work focuses on baryonic feedback: the heating and expulsion of gas by exploding stars and supermassive black holes, which is now one of the largest systematic uncertainties limiting cosmology on small scales. I aim to turn small-scale structure from a systematic into a cosmological probe by combining fast physical models (such as my Baryon Pasting model), large suites of hydrodynamic simulations (such as CAMELS), and multi-wavelength survey data from X-ray, microwave, and weak-lensing surveys, including eROSITA, XRISM, the Simons Observatory, and LSST.

Before Nara, I was a Visiting Scientist at the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian, a Postdoctoral Associate at the University of Miami, and a postdoc and Associate Research Scientist at Yale University. I received my Ph.D. in Astronomy & Astrophysics from the University of Chicago in 2010.
