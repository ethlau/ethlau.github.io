research

Baryonic feedback and small-scale structure as a cosmological probe

Baryonic feedback is now one of the largest systematic uncertainties limiting cosmology on small scales. My research program addresses it by combining fast physical models, thousands of simulations, and multi-probe survey data (X-ray, microwave, and weak lensing), turning small-scale structure from a systematic into a cosmological probe in the era of eROSITA, XRISM, the Simons Observatory, and LSST. The approach has three steps: quantify astrophysical systematics with simulations, build fast forward models that predict multi-wavelength observables, and confront them with survey data using simulation-based inference and machine learning.

Research interests

  • Computational, theoretical, and statistical modeling of galaxy clusters, groups, and massive galaxies, and their observational signatures in X-ray and microwave
  • Galaxy clusters and groups as cosmological probes
  • Physical processes in the intracluster medium: gas motions, turbulence, and non-thermal pressure
  • Diffuse emission from the intergalactic and circumgalactic medium
  • Astronomical software development

Current projects

Baryon Pasting. Baryon Pasting (BP) is a fast model I developed that adds realistic gas physics (cooling, star formation, supernova and AGN feedback) to dark-matter-only simulations. Applied to the 2.1 Gpc Uchuu simulation, it produced X-ray and Sunyaev–Zel’dovich (SZ) maps for 75 million halos (Lau et al. 2025). I have since made it differentiable, so that cosmology and feedback parameters can be fit directly to survey data. Through the Baryon Factory effort, I am applying BP to a range of simulations (Backlight, AGORA, PINOCCHIO, Mucho-Uchuu) and to the Local Volume Complete Cluster Survey (LoVoCCS).

eROSITA: cosmology and feedback. I led the first measurements of the X-ray angular power spectrum of groups and clusters in eFEDS and eRASS1 (Lau et al. 2023, 2025), obtaining S8 = 0.80+0.02−0.01, consistent with the early Universe. In the CAMELS circumgalactic-medium project, comparing eROSITA data with thousands of simulations showed that existing feedback models over- or under-predict the hot gas around galaxies (Lau et al. 2025). As a member of the FornaX Collaboration, I use the Euclid Flagship simulations to model how baryonic physics shapes the selection and scaling-relation scatter of groups and clusters in the FornaX X-ray survey.

XRISM: gas motions and non-thermal pressure. Building on my interpretation of the Hitomi observations of the Perseus cluster (Lau et al. 2017), I confront XRISM/Resolve data with cluster simulations passed through the same analysis chain as the data, including a study of non-thermal pressure with TNG-Cluster (Lau, Ota & Nagai 2026) and an analysis of sloshing-driven gas motions in Abell 2029 (Uchida et al. 2026). I lead the simulation and theory component of a XRISM Key Project proposal led by Prof. Naomi Ota.

Does baryonic feedback explain the S8 tension? The clumpiness of matter predicted from the cosmic microwave background is higher than what late-time measurements such as weak lensing find. Feedback from stars and black holes smooths the matter distribution on small scales and could explain part of this gap. I am combining Baryon Pasting with simulation-based inference and applying it to eROSITA X-ray data for more than ten thousand optically selected groups and clusters, to measure how much feedback the data allow and whether it is enough to reconcile early- and late-time S8.

Earlier work

At Chicago and Yale, I showed that residual gas motions bias hydrostatic cluster masses through non-thermal pressure (Lau et al. 2009, 2013; Nelson et al. 2014), predicted merger-induced gas motions (Nagai et al. 2013), and characterized cluster shapes (Lau et al. 2011, 2012, 2021) and gas clumping and accretion in cluster outskirts (Nagai & Lau 2011; Lau et al. 2015; Aung, Nagai & Lau 2021).
